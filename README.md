# Forest Detectives: The Mystery of the Forest Parcel

A family-friendly detective adventure for players aged 7–12. Choose one of five forest heroes, solve the mystery of a missing parcel, help the residents of a growing woodland village, and explore dangerous regions filled with animal, insect, and bird robots.

The game combines story quests, exploration, light action combat, puzzles, gathering, crafting, and local save slots. Progress can be approached at a comfortable pace, and most village activities are optional.

## Game Features

- **Five playable detectives.** Play as Wolf Cub, Fox Cub, Rabbit, Watermelon, or Sheepwolf. Each hero has different attributes, a signature tool, and a personal home challenge.
- **Animated encounters and inhabitants.** Directional walking, weapon swings, wingbeats, and attack warnings share a scene-time animation controller. Ordinary inhabitants take short walks and stop to talk; shops, quest givers, and royal guards remain easy to find.
- **A chapter-based adventure.** Investigate the missing parcel, help the Forest Village, cross two mountain regions, then explore a snowy route to the Ice Palace and its throne room.
- **A large connected world.** Explore the Forest Village, Wild Forest, Aunt Melon's Farm, Forest Mine, Mountain Hollow, Uncle Mole's Shop, five hero homes, and Beaver's trap-filled house.
- **Village quests and resident errands.** Recover lost letters, collect robot parts, defeat forest machines, repair the village, water flowerbeds, deliver baked goods, light lamps, and help residents in the western district.
- **Melee and ranged combat.** Use each hero's main weapon or switch to throwable tomatoes and cucumbers. Enemy health, rank, and combat status are displayed directly in the world.
- **Four enemy ranks.** Weak, normal, and strong robots return after different delays, while bosses remain defeated. Every recurring robot can drop a new physical gear after each victory.
- **Mountain encounters.** Fight robot beetles, wasps, and mantises before facing two bear guardians with different weapons and attack patterns.
- **Bird Pass combat.** Face 18 feather-firing robot sparrows, owls, and hawks across a `4800×1600` mountain route. Metal feathers are physical projectiles that break against cover and can be blocked or reflected.
- **A three-phase turtle boss.** Armor Shell alternates between feather barrages with an exposed core, invulnerable rolling attacks that must hit energy pylons, and rotating feather storms with marked falling volleys.
- **Gadgets with practical uses.** Buy a jetpack, magnetic glove, gas mask, and pulse shield from Uncle Mole. The shield blocks feathers, while the glove creates a 1.5-second field that can reflect the first three captured projectiles.
- **Resources and crafting.** Gather stones, sticks, ropes, and mechanical scrap. Craft a pickaxe and use it to mine iron and diamonds in a mine whose entrance and ore layout change with every new game.
- **A safe farm area.** Visit Aunt Melon's Farm, meet its watermelon and melon residents, buy throwable vegetables, and explore without enemy encounters. Watermelon starts the adventure in the central garden bed.
- **Beaver's challenge house.** Cross a collapsing floor, survive wall launchers and poison gas, solve a power-door mechanism, and use a jetpack to reach the final control panel. Room checkpoints preserve completed stages.
- **Optional home challenges.** Visit the heroes' homes for lasso practice, mirror puzzles, evidence sorting, greenhouse controls, and batting training.
- **Healing potions.** Every hero carries three potions. Each restores 33% of maximum health and recharges independently after 90 seconds.
- **Four adjustable difficulties.** Switch between Walk, Story, Hard, and Impossible at any time from the pause screen. Enemy attacks deal no damage on Walk, 20% of base damage on Story, 97.5% on Hard, and 150% on Impossible. Enemy health and trap damage keep their separate existing scaling; rewards do not change.
- **A snowy third chapter.** Defeating Armor Shell ends chapter two. Returning to the village begins chapter three and adds camera-following snowfall to all outdoor locations while unfinished village activities remain available.
- **A new winter route.** Travel from the village through Snow Valley and Snow City, break the siege outside Krok City, visit its eight peaceful districts, then enter the Ice Palace and a separate throne room. Fight snowballs, snowmen, robot albatrosses, snow golems, and stronger ice golems.
- **A city under siege.** Defeat six snowmen, four ice golems, and three ice catapults outside the crocodile fortress. Friendly armored guards and catapults exchange harmless background fire while you fight. Clearing all thirteen attackers opens the gates permanently and awards a defender's badge and eight gears.
- **Krok City and its market.** Explore a `9600×3200` city with integrated residential, craftsmen's, market and royal neighbourhoods, varied architecture, twelve named neighbours, four optional errands, a guarded Prince, and two merchants. Residents walk between their homes and local streets; the upper and lower avenues connect every district. Buy stronger throwable vegetables or instantly refill missing healing-potion charges for two gears each.
- **The Walrus at the throne.** Approach to start a three-stage boss fight. Dodge icicle volleys, avoid the warned tail sweep, and keep clear of tusk strikes. Ice furniture can block projectiles, and no gadget is required. Victory begins chapter four and ends the snowfall; returning to the village reveals a flood and opens the ocean storyline. The winter maps remain open.
- **Local saving and loading.** Use an autosave and three manual slots. Saves include quests, health, inventory, purchases, gathered resources, mine generation, enemy respawn timers, dropped loot, defeated bosses, and Beaver House checkpoints.

## Adventure Structure

### The Missing Parcel

The first chapter introduces the selected detective and the central mystery. Search for clues, question characters, and determine what happened to the parcel. Completing the case opens the road to the Forest Village.

### The Forest Village

The second chapter is built around four main assignments that can be accepted and completed in parallel:

1. Recover three lost letters for the Squirrel Postie.
2. Bring five robot parts to Beaver.
3. Disable ten forest robots for the Owl Guardian.
4. Enter Beaver's house and shut down its malfunctioning defense system.

Turning in all four assignments unlocks the **Village Saved** milestone. It does not end the chapter: the main route continues through Mountain Hollow and Bird Pass. Resident errands and hero-home challenges remain optional sources of gears, keepsakes, and additional conversations.

### Bird Pass and Chapter Three

Defeating both bear guardians opens the eastern gate to Bird Pass. The pass has its own cumulative victory counter and recurring gear drops. At the eastern arena, defeat the three-phase robot turtle Armor Shell to complete chapter two. The reward includes eight gears and the Bird Pass Badge. The hero may stay to explore or return directly to the village, where chapter three begins with snow across every outdoor region.

### The Winter Route and Chapter Four

In chapter three, a western village exit leads through Snow Valley and Snow City to the outskirts of Krok City. All thirteen besiegers must be defeated to open the city gates; siege enemies never respawn. The peaceful city has four optional tasks: recover four rivets, find three tablets, deliver three medical kits, and light four lamps. Each pays four gears when turned in to its quest giver. The Krok Prince, protected by four spear guards, asks you to defeat the Walrus. Accepting his assignment opens the east passage to the Ice Palace without requiring any city errands. The Walrus attacks when approached. Defeating him grants the Ice Palace Badge, restores health, and immediately begins chapter four. You can later return to the Prince for eight gears and the Krok Seal, but this turn-in is optional. Chapter four continues with the ocean adventure below, while existing quests remain available.

### The Alarming Tide — Chapter Four

After defeating the Walrus, return to the village. Its outskirts are flooded. The Owl meets the hero on the central square; accepting her explanation opens a northern road to the **Beach**. The introductory conversation occurs once, including when continuing an older post-Walrus save.

The Beach, Sea and Trench are each **4800×1600**. Twenty melee robots (twelve crabs and eight albatrosses) inhabit the Beach. Each drops one gear, and two scrap piles provide ten gears each: a complete first exploration can fund the forty-gear scuba purchase without waiting for respawns. Beach albatrosses have no feather attacks; their snowy relatives keep their original attacks.

**Visit the Beach first**, then have at least **40 gears** in the wallet to automatically receive “Return to Mole”. Earlier savings count. The objective remains if money is later spent, but Mole checks the current balance before selling the scuba set. Buy it by talking to Mole. Scuba always costs **40**, works passively, has unlimited air, does not replace the active gadget and survives defeat.

Scuba opens the Sea, containing predatory fish and three schools of neutral jellyfish. Contact or an attack provokes only that school; even a lethal first hit alerts the other members. Aggression and defeated members survive saving and travel. A school respawns neutral as a group sixty seconds after its last member is defeated. Marine wildlife drops no gears.

The **Tiger Shark** guards the eastern end of the Sea, with warned bite dashes and a tail sweep; below half health it chains two separately warned dashes. Its permanent defeat opens the Trench, inhabited by stronger spiny prehistoric fish. **Ichthyosaur** guards an ancient tide machine and uses three phases of sonic rings with a safe gap, marked surfacing attacks and a pulling whirlpool. Defeat it, then interact with the machine to stop the flood and earn the Coast Saviour badge. The village dries out and the marine route remains open; chapter five is not started.

Roads use continuous panoramic artwork and connected collision corridors. Automated checks cover actual keyboard movement through the northern village road and marine junctions in both directions, alongside the existing forest, mountain, snow and Krok passage regressions.

## Enemies and Respawning

| Rank | Examples | Respawn Time |
|---|---|---:|
| Weak | Robot hares and snowballs | 30 seconds |
| Normal | Robot wolves, beetles, wasps, sparrows, and snowmen | 60 seconds |
| Strong | Robot boars, mantises, owls, hawks, robot albatrosses, and snow or ice golems | 90 seconds |
| Boss | Reinforced boar, bear guardians, Armor Shell, and the Walrus | No respawn |

Forest quest progress uses a cumulative victory counter, so robots defeated after respawning continue to count. Mountain Hollow, Bird Pass, Snow Valley, Snow City, and the Ice Palace each have separate counters and do not affect the Owl or Beaver assignments. Existing robots continue to leave location-specific gear drops that can be collected with `E`; on the new winter route, only robot albatrosses drop gears. Siege snowmen, golems, and catapults are permanent one-time enemies with no individual gear drops; their shared reward comes when all thirteen are defeated.

## Equipment and Economy

All ordinary shop prices use the highest reached chapter: the parcel prologue and first village adventure keep their base prices, the winter chapter multiplies them by **1.5**, and the ocean chapter by **2.25**. Round each result upward once from the base price. This applies even when returning to an older shop. Gadget/material purchases and vegetable packs share this calculation; pharmacy refills multiply the rounded price of one charge by the missing charge count. Scuba is the fixed-price **40-gear** exception. Prices in the tables below are the **base prices**.

Gears are earned from robots, quests, errands, hidden scrap, and home challenges. Rewards and purchases are one-time, so the game does not require repeated reward farming.

| Gadget | Price | Use with `Q` |
|---|---:|---|
| Jetpack | 12 gears | Automatically crosses a marked chasm from its launch pad |
| Magnetic Glove | 8 gears | Pulls mechanisms; on Bird Pass it captures feathers for 1.5 seconds and reflects up to three |
| Gas Mask | 8 gears | Protects from gas for 8 seconds; 12-second cooldown |
| Pulse Shield | 10 gears | Blocks the next hit or volley for 2 seconds; 6-second cooldown |

Aunt Melon sells two tomatoes or two cucumbers for one gear. Thrown vegetables deal light damage: four hits are needed to defeat a standard robot hare. When vegetable ammunition runs out, the hero returns to their main weapon automatically.

Krok City's northern market street sells two Dense Tomatoes for three gears (1.5 damage each) or two Large Cucumbers for four gears (2 damage each). The pharmacist on the southern market street refills only missing potion charges up to three at two gears per charge; this does not restore health. Normal 90-second automatic potion recharge still works. The main weapon and all stocked vegetable types cycle with `R`.

Beaver also sells safe training versions of a collapsing floor kit and a falling wall kit. Purchased building materials are recorded in the rewards section of the journal.

## Controls

- `WASD` or arrow keys — move.
- `Shift` — run while stamina is available.
- `E` — interact, talk, mine, or collect an item.
- `Space` — attack in combat areas or perform an action in certain challenges.
- `R` — cycle between the main weapon and every vegetable type currently in stock.
- `Q` — use the equipped gadget.
- `T` — use a healing potion.
- `I` — open the journal, inventory, resources, rewards, gadgets, and saves.
- `Esc` — close the current window; when no window is open, pause the world and choose a difficulty.

Character dialogue appears along the bottom of the screen. Short instructions, combat warnings, and system notifications appear in a compact panel on the right.

## Run Locally

The project requires Node.js 24 LTS.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Vite prints the local game address after startup. To create and preview a production build:

```bash
npm run build
npm run preview
```

Useful scene shortcuts for visual testing:

```text
?scene=forest-village
?scene=wild-forest
?scene=mountain-hollow
?scene=bird-pass
?scene=snow-valley
?scene=snow-city
?scene=krok-outskirts
?scene=krok-city
?scene=ice-palace
?scene=ice-throne
?scene=beach
?scene=sea
?scene=trench
?scene=forest-mine
?scene=melon-farm
?scene=mole-shop
?scene=beaver-house
?scene=hero-home
?scene=animation-gallery
```

Add a hero query when needed, for example `&hero=watermelon`.

The development-only animation gallery lets you select an actor, action, and facing, preview moving attacks, pause a clip, and inspect its frames. Heroes use transparent torso and leg parts for continuous opposing strides: each foot moves forward and backward, with a lifted return step. Gait follows actual movement and stops at obstacles; it does not blend or blur full-body images. Combat actions retain their authored frame animations. World art loads only for actors present in the current location; portraits keep their original artwork and visual movement never resizes collision bodies. The journal and pause screen freeze world animations, projectiles, and combat timers. A melee swing lands after a 120 ms windup and retains its 450 ms cooldown. Animation state and resident patrol positions are transient: existing save files keep their current version and progress.

Forest Village and Snow Valley use single continuous panoramic backgrounds instead of independently drawn halves. Krok City uses complete painted neighbourhood backgrounds: buildings, courtyards, snowbanks and warm lamplight belong to the same scene rather than separate building sprites on rectangular plots. Pavement transitions are blended at district boundaries. Projecting roofs appear in front of actors walking behind houses, with the hidden hero remaining faintly visible. All eight districts retain connected upper, lower and cross-streets, with solid building footprints and local resident walks.

Locomotion uses collision-resolved physics steps rather than individual screen refreshes, so 120/144 Hz displays do not alternate between idle, walking, and running. Visual positions and camera tracking are interpolated without moving collision bodies. Walk/run transitions keep the stride phase, and the run sheets use a fixed per-character scale calibration to avoid sudden size changes.

## Project Structure

- `src/domain` — characters, quests, economy, resources, gadgets, enemies, saves, and game state.
- `src/game/scenes` — locations, combat areas, shops, homes, puzzles, and UI scenes.
- `src/game/animation` — actor clips, action markers, fixed-footprint rendering, and resident patrols.
- `public/assets/animations` — transparent directional animation sheets.
- `public/assets/maps` — Tiled object maps with collisions, roads, NPCs, portals, resources, and encounter points.
- `public/assets` — character, enemy, NPC, gadget, interior, and world artwork.
- `tests/unit` and `tests/e2e` — domain tests and browser-based game scenarios.

The current session and save slots are stored in the browser. Cloud synchronization and save transfer between browsers are not included.

### Ocean update validation and saves

Save version 5 reads versions 1–4 without discarding currency, equipment, prior quests or defeated bosses. Ocean story milestones, scuba purchases, boss victories and school state trigger autosaves. Older post-Walrus saves receive the pending flood introduction on their next village entry.

Ocean artwork is stored in `public/assets/ocean/`; generation prompts and provenance are recorded in `ASSET_OCEAN_PROMPTS.md`.
